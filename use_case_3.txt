EU data protection law, primarily the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), establishes several key obligations regarding data minimization and user consent.  These are crucial aspects of ensuring the protection of personal data within the European Union.

**Data Minimization:**

The GDPR's principle of data minimization, outlined in Article 5(1)(c), mandates that personal data collected should be "adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary" for the specified purpose.  This means that only the data absolutely required for the intended processing activity should be collected.  Collecting excessive or unnecessary data is prohibited.  The regulation doesn't explicitly detail specific methods for data minimization, but it implies the need for careful consideration of the purpose and the least intrusive means to achieve it.  This aligns with the broader principle of data protection by design and by default (Article 25), which encourages organizations to integrate data protection considerations at all stages of the design and development process, including the choice of data storage and processing methods.

**User Consent:**

The GDPR's requirements regarding user consent are detailed in Article 7.  Consent must be freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous.  This means that the data subject (the user) must actively agree to the processing of their data, not simply through inaction or pre-selected boxes.  The information provided must be clear, concise, and easily understood, enabling users to make truly informed decisions.  Article 7 also explicitly grants users the right to withdraw their consent at any time without impacting the legality of prior processing based on that consent.  The process of obtaining consent must be documented.  This includes recording what was consented to, and the demonstration of how and when this consent was given.  

Articles 13 and 14 further elaborate on the information that must be provided to data subjects when their data is collected, including the purposes of processing, the legal basis for it, and the recipients of the data.  This comprehensive approach ensures transparency and facilitates informed consent.  The scope of consent must be clearly defined and cover all processing activities undertaken for the stated purpose(s).  Where multiple purposes exist, consent must be given for each. Specific articles such as Article 6(1) define legitimate processing purposes that don't always necessitate consent, but in other cases, explicit consent from the data subject is a key requirement.  Failure to meet these standards could result in serious penalties, including administrative fines.

In summary, EU data protection law places considerable emphasis on both data minimization and informed user consent to safeguard the privacy and rights of individuals within the Union.  Strict adherence to the articles referenced above is essential for all organizations processing personal data within the EU's jurisdiction.
