These rights are centered around the principles of legality, legitimacy, necessity, and good faith in the processing of personal information.  Crucially, the law mandates that personal information be processed only with the individual's consent, unless specific exceptions apply (such as when necessary for contract performance, statutory duties, or public health emergencies).

One significant right is the ability to **withdraw consent**. Article 15 of the PIPL explicitly outlines this right, allowing individuals to revoke previously granted permission for the processing of their personal information.  This right is particularly important for maintaining control over one's data and ensuring that processing remains aligned with the individual's wishes.  Additionally, individuals have the right to be informed about how their personal information is being processed (Articles 7 and 17 highlight the principles of openness, transparency, and notification).

The PIPL also provides recourse in cases of infringement.  If an individual's rights are violated due to personal information processing, and the processor cannot demonstrate lack of fault, they are liable for damages (Article 69).  The amount of compensation is determined by the court based on the losses suffered or benefits gained by the processor, or based on the specific circumstances if those amounts are difficult to ascertain.  Furthermore, individuals can file complaints or reports regarding illegal processing with the relevant departments (Article 65), who are obligated to process these promptly and inform the complainant or reporter of the outcome.  In cases of widespread infringement affecting many individuals, legal action can be initiated by the people's procuratorate, consumer organizations, or organizations designated by the State Cyberspace Administration (Article 70).

Specific protections are also afforded to minors.  If a personal information processor is aware that they are handling data of a minor under 14, they must obtain consent from the minor's parent or guardian (Article 31).  This highlights a heightened level of protection for children's personal information.  Finally, it's important to note that the law does not apply to personal information processing by a natural person for their own or family affairs (Article 72).
